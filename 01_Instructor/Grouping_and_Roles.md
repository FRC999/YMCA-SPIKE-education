# Groups and jobs for 12 children

Use four teams of three for grades 5–6. Two teams work with the robots while two teams work at separate, supervised building tables. **A navigator is a job on a robot team, not the building station.** Children change jobs; nobody spends the whole session watching or writing.

## Team assignments

Put actual names on a local attendance sheet, not in this repository. Give each child an A, B or C position within their team.

| Team | Three child positions | Shared kit and lane | First activity |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | T1-A, T1-B, T1-C | Kit A | Robot |
| T2 | T2-A, T2-B, T2-C | Kit A | Build table A |
| T3 | T3-A, T3-B, T3-C | Kit B | Robot |
| T4 | T4-A, T4-B, T4-C | Kit B | Build table B |

## Jobs at the robot

| Job | Exactly what the child does |
|---|---|
| **Programmer / driver** | Makes the agreed edit or chooses the tested routine. Says what changed. Starts only after the navigator says the lane is clear. Uses the demonstrated Stop control. The driver does not pick up a moving robot. |
| **Robot handler / navigator** | Reads the next step and says a prediction. When the program is stopped, positions the robot and payload at the start, fits a mentor-approved attachment, and resets after the run. Checks the lane and calls “clear.” Keeps hands out during motion. |
| **Test observer** | Watches what actually happens. Reads the ruler, display or sensor result. Records **one number, tick or short phrase**, or dictates it to the mentor. Suggests one next test. This child does not write a report while the others play. |

All three suggest ideas and predict. The observer has first responsibility for the result, but everyone watches the test. The mentor handles connection problems and support-code repairs so the children's turn stays useful.

## Jobs at the build table

| Job | Exactly what the child does |
|---|---|
| **Builder** | Leads the current assembly step. Each child connects pieces; the lead builder does not do the whole build alone. |
| **Designer / parts manager** | Reads the short challenge, helps choose a plan, finds and counts parts, and checks the size against the mounting or clearance template. |
| **Tester / reporter** | Performs the hand test, checks stability and clearance, describes the result, and records a tick or a few words. Proposes one improvement. |

The build challenge has its own physical result. It is not a worksheet version of the robot exercise. Use the [eight build station cards](../02_Student/Station_Task_Cards.md).

## How jobs rotate

Use the same three-place rotation at both stations. Move the job cards, not the children between distant tables, at a role change.

| Phase | Child A | Child B | Child C |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Driver / Builder | Navigator / Designer | Observer / Tester |
| Second | Observer / Tester | Driver / Builder | Navigator / Designer |
| Third | Navigator / Designer | Observer / Tester | Driver / Builder |

At the robot, use the job before the slash. At the build table, use the job after it. Rotate every **four minutes** during the 12-minute turns and every **two minutes** during the six-minute robot retests. Stop motion before a handoff. If a trial is delayed, the next child still gets the controls; do not let technical trouble erase their turn.

## Normal hour for Weeks 1–7

| Minutes | T1 and T3 | T2 and T4 | Role-change times |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome and introductions | Welcome and introductions | Week 1: names, mentors and stop signal; no opening quiz |
| 5–10 | Short demonstration | Short demonstration | Hub-light reminder |
| 10–14 | Read the robot rule and predict | Read the robot rule and predict | Combined code/prediction slide |
| 14–26 | Robot | Build | 18 and 22 |
| 26–38 | Build | Robot | 30 and 34 |
| 38–44 | Robot retest with approved team build | Finish or improve build | 40 and 42 |
| 44–50 | Finish or improve build | Robot retest with approved team build | 46 and 48 |
| 50–54 | Individual reflection within Kit A/B pods | Individual reflection within Kit A/B pods | Each child contributes |
| 54–60 | Save, count, pack and dismiss | Save, count, pack and dismiss | Leave teaching bases assembled |

Each team has **18 scheduled minutes at a robot**. Equal job rotation gives each child about **six minutes as driver**, plus active handling, observation and building. These are scheduled opportunities, not a guarantee of uninterrupted motor time. Avoid whole-class queues for a single robot.

The first robot teams have not built their own attachment yet. Put a **mentor-tested example at each robot**. They can test the example or bare base first, then use their own approved build during the retest. Store four team builds in labeled trays. Do not make a team's first robot turn depend on another team finishing a build.

## Mentors and laptops

Four student instructors cover Robot A, Robot B, Build A and Build B. A fifth can troubleshoot equipment; a sixth can support individual children, help with transitions and check participation. The adult lead retains overall supervision and YMCA procedures. A floater does not replace the adult.

Bring all four available laptops. Keep two connected to the robots. Use the other two for build references, viewing the current card or documenting the model when useful; they do not connect to the shared hubs. Print the cards as well so the build activity does not depend on a screen or network. Two laptops can still run the class with printed build cards.

Place building tables away from the driving lanes and facing their own materials. Each has its own parts, example, timer and mentor. Give a visible finish test and a small extension. If children stop doing anything useful for two minutes, shorten the directions, offer two design choices or introduce the hand test. Do not solve disengagement by assigning more writing.

## Showcase exception

Week 8 uses its own schedule: 0–7 individual final check; 7–12 briefing and prediction; 12–24 two six-minute robot rehearsals per pod while the other team finishes its exhibit; 24–28 setup; 28–44 four four-minute demonstrations; 44–50 celebrate designs and evidence; 50–54 reflection; 54–60 cleanup. During a six-minute rehearsal, each child drives for one two-minute phase. Each child also has a speaking or showing part in the demonstration.

## One-minute mentor check after class

For each child, record whether they built, operated and explained something. Ask what was fun or surprising and what they would change. Use actual observations to improve the next session. A working robot alone does not establish that all children participated.

# Two hour trainer session for Week 1

Use this plan for the student-trainer session on October 25, 2026. The first assignment is to **assemble and test both SPIKE teaching robots**. Trainers then practice the first lesson and teach it to one another. The adult lead gives the brief, checks readiness, and coaches when needed.

**Give each trainer group the [student trainer work orders](Trainer_Work_Orders.md).** Those instructions contain the reading links, build steps, tests, and completion records. Keep this facilitator plan for yourself.

The [instructor guide](Instructor_and_Mentor_Guide.docx), under **Train the high school mentors**, and the [mentor workshop presentation](../03_Presentations/00_Mentor_Workshop.pptx) already contain a 120-minute workshop. That workshop samples the full eight-week course. Today’s plan replaces its agenda with more build time and a focused rehearsal of Week 1. The [Week 1 detailed notes](W01_Detailed_Speaker_Notes.html) and [Week 1 presentation with notes](../03_Presentations/W01_signal_with_speaker_notes.pptx) are the teaching materials the trainers will rehearse.

## Outcomes by the end

- Two assembled, labeled SPIKE driving bases, with a separate test record for each.
- A working Week 1 starter on each teaching device, or a specific blocker with an owner and completion date.
- Every trainer has operated a robot, explained the three-line recipe, and practiced coaching a beginner.
- Named responsibilities for the first session, including setup, the R3PTAR demonstration, kit stations, and cleanup.

A completed checklist records what actually passed. Do not label a robot ready because its assembly is finished.

## Prepare before trainers arrive

1. Put Kit A and Kit B on separate tables, with their own trays. Leave the parts for the trainers to assemble. Start charging the hubs early.
2. Provide two teaching computers, one per kit, with SPIKE App 3 available and permission to connect to the hubs. Open the repository or download it in advance. Keep the complete Python files accessible as text.
3. Open the [assembly PDF](../06_References/LEGO_Practice_Driving_Base.pdf) on each table’s device. Have a separate viewing device or a printed copy if changing between the app and PDF will be awkward.
4. Put tape, labels, pencils, rulers, and the [work orders](Trainer_Work_Orders.md) at each table. Print one copy of the completion record per kit, or have the groups record results in their own copies.
5. Mark two separate floor test lanes. Set aside a stable support that lifts both drive wheels clear for the first tests.
6. Have the actual app and hub stop procedure ready to demonstrate. Resolve installation permissions yourself; trainers should not spend the workshop waiting for an installer.
7. Keep personal spare parts separate and use the [loan log](../SESSION_PREP.md#personal-parts-loan-log) for anything borrowed.

Do not make R3PTAR assembly a competing first task today. Assign its remaining work at the end, after the two teaching bases are ready or their blockers are documented. Distance/color sensor exercises and the later-week attachment are follow-up training.

## Launch instructions to say aloud

“Your first job is to get two teaching robots assembled and tested for our first YMCA session. Each table has a work order with the exact instructions and files. Work through it, rotate jobs, and bring me the robot and evidence at the checkpoints. I will help with a blocker, but I want you to use the instructions, inspect your work, and explain what you found. Later, each of you will teach a short piece of the lesson to another trainer.”

Keep this introduction to five minutes. Show the two kit labels and the work-order link. Assign groups, identify who to call for help, then let them start.

## Two hour agenda

Times are elapsed minutes from the start. The two kit groups work in parallel.

| Minutes | Trainers do | Adult lead does | Evidence to see |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Open the work orders; assign roles and Kit A/B | Give the brief and identify stop/help procedure | Everyone knows their first job |
| 5–45 | Work order 1: inventory essentials, assemble, label, trace cables | Check progress around minute 25 and 35; inspect completed builds | Two chassis; no wheel/cable interference; A/B wiring traced |
| 45–65 | Work order 2: connect, smoke-test, run Week 1 starter | Approve raised-wheel setup and stop controls before motion; observe each floor test | Separate recorded results for both kits |
| 65–80 | Work order 3: each trainer edits and predicts the Week 1 recipe | Ask for explanations rather than typing fixes | Each trainer runs; longer pause correctly explained |
| 80–90 | Work order 4: read assigned notes and prepare a mini lesson | Assign teaching order and check that the explanations fit beginners | Short teaching plan and prediction question |
| 90–110 | Work order 5: teach, observe, give feedback, swap | Visit both groups and listen to each trainer | Each trainer teaches; feedback identifies one useful improvement |
| 110–120 | Work order 6: save, record readiness, assign first-session jobs | Review both kit records, unresolved issues, and individual readiness | Named owners; files saved; robots left assembled |

## Group roles and participation

Use two kit groups. With four trainers, put two at each table. With six to eight trainers, put three or four at each table. With only two trainers, assign one to each kit and have them cross-check one another at the checkpoints; you act as the second set of eyes during tests.

During building, rotate builder, instruction reader, checker, and recorder every ten minutes. Combine roles in smaller groups. Do not let the most experienced builder assemble the entire robot while everyone else watches. During programming, change the operator after every test, and make sure every trainer gets a turn.

The twenty-minute teaching block provides up to four five-minute turns per kit group: three minutes teaching and two minutes feedback. With fewer than four trainers per group, use remaining turns for a repeat after feedback or a troubleshooting scenario. With more than eight trainers total, schedule extra observed teaching time before assigning unobserved trainers to lead a kit station; do not silently shorten everyone’s practice to a few seconds.

## Your checkpoints

### Before the first powered test

Ask the group to show the robot’s front, trace the left wheel motor to A and the right wheel motor to B, and explain which motor is unused. Verify wheel clearance and secure loose cables. Confirm that the intended hub is connected and that the group can identify and operate the actual stop controls. Begin with both wheels raised.

### Before accepting a kit as Week 1 ready

Observe a short floor run on each kit. The robot must show the requested letter, wait, and move straight in the labeled forward direction. Watch the group change the pause from 500 to 1000, predict a longer wait, and rerun without changing drive(90). Check that they save a working project and know how to restore the clean starter.

Ask: “Which observations belong to this kit?” A successful run on Kit A does not certify Kit B.

### Before assigning a trainer to lead students

Ask each trainer to explain one recipe line, name the stop procedure, and demonstrate a coaching question. Look for accurate units, a prediction before Run, a role change, and a chance for the learner to answer. A trainer needing more practice can assist a ready mentor and repeat the check before leading independently.

## If assembly or connection takes longer

At minute 35, ask each group to report its current build step and any missing part. If a part is missing, record its description and step number; use a logged compatible replacement only if it preserves the build’s function.

If both groups need fifteen more minutes, use this recovery schedule: finish builds by minute 60; test from 60–75; practice the recipe from 75–85; prepare teaching from 85–90; retain teaching practice at 90–110 and handoff at 110–120. Shorten explanations and optional repetition, not the basic test or each trainer’s teaching turn.

If a robot is still blocked at minute 75, stop consuming the entire workshop on it. Rehearse with the working kit, or use the code cards if neither kit works. Record the exact blocker, owner, and next check time. That kit remains **not ready** until its tests pass. Do not announce that both robots are ready based on a paper exercise.
